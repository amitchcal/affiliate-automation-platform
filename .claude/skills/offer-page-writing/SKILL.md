# Writing offer pages and articles

## What the page is for

A visitor arrives from a pin, a search, or an ad, knowing little. The page's job is to tell them honestly what the product is, what they get, and whether it suits them, then send the interested ones to the seller. The seller's page does the selling. Ours earns trust by being plain and accurate, and that is also what keeps it compliant.

Read the affiliate-compliance skill first. This skill covers structure and style; that one covers what may be said.

## Research before writing

1. Open the seller's sales page through the affiliate link, and the seller's affiliate page.
2. List the facts the seller states: format, what is included, who made it, refund terms.
3. Keep the source of each fact. A draft must be able to say where every claim came from (story S-02).
4. Note the seller's strong claims so you can leave them out: promised results, time frames, testimonials, scarcity.
5. If a fact cannot be found on the seller's material, do not write it. A plausible guess is a false statement.

## Offer page structure

Title: `<Product name>: Overview`. Summary: one or two sentences, shown under the title and on the home page.

The disclosure box is added by the page template. Do not write one in the body.

| Section | Content |
|---|---|
| Opening paragraph | What it is and who it is for, in two or three sentences |
| Button | `[[cta:See it on the official site]]` |
| At a glance | Format; method; who created it; price as a link to the seller, never a figure; refund terms, attributed |
| What it covers | The product's structure or contents, in the seller's own terms |
| What is included | A list of the components |
| Button | Second call to action |
| Who it suits | Two or three kinds of people, specific |
| Who should look elsewhere | Honest and specific. Include cases that need a professional. |
| A caution | The seller's page makes strong promises; results depend on the person and on consistent use |
| About the creator | Credentials, attributed to the seller |
| Common questions | Three or four: format, price (link only), refund, one specific to the product |
| How we prepared this overview | The fixed wording below |
| Button | Final call to action |

Fixed wording for the last section, unless the product really was used:

> This page summarizes information published by the product's creator. It is an overview, not a hands-on review: we have not used the product ourselves. Please confirm the details on the official page before you buy.

## Markdown the site supports

The renderer escapes all HTML, so only these work:

- `## Heading` and `### Sub-heading`
- `- item` and `1. item` lists
- `**bold**`
- `[text](https://address)` and `[text](/same-site-page)`
- `[[cta:Button label]]` on its own line, for the affiliate button

Never put the affiliate link in the body. The button uses the page's tracked link, which is how clicks are counted.

## Style

- American English. The audience is in the US.
- Plain words, short sentences, no hype. If a sentence could appear in an advertisement, rewrite it.
- Second person for the reader. "We" only for the site.
- Specific over general: "21 training games" is better than "lots of content", when the seller states the number.
- Button labels say what happens: "See the course on the official site", not "Buy now" or "Get instant access".
- Length: 400 to 700 words. A reader should finish it in three minutes.

## Articles

An article earns a visit by being useful on its own, then points to an overview.

1. Answer one specific question the audience asks ("How to stop a puppy from biting").
2. Give general, accurate guidance in numbered steps. No product in the main body.
3. Say what not to do, and when to consult a professional.
4. Close with one paragraph linking to the relevant overview page as one option among others.

The article template adds the disclosure line. Do not make medical, veterinary, legal, or financial claims; write general information and say so.

## Before handing over a draft

- Every fact has a source in the seller's material.
- The page passes the wording check, and the seller's banned claims are absent.
- "Who should look elsewhere" would stop the wrong buyer.
- There is no price figure, testimonial, rating, or guarantee of results.
- Save it as a draft. Publishing follows the brand's approval setting; never publish on your own unless that setting allows it.
